# LinkedIn Post Structural Template

Reverse-engineered from how McKinsey, Gartner, Deloitte and BCG actually structure informative LinkedIn posts. Scribe writes to this template; Sentinel checks against it.

1. **Hook** — 140–210 characters, must work as a standalone claim before LinkedIn's "…see more" cutoff. A stat, a contrarian claim, or a sharp question — never a throat-clearing opener.
2. **Tension** — 2–3 short lines naming the gap the reader feels but hasn't articulated.
3. **Thesis** — one sentence, on its own line. This is the line someone would screenshot. Everything before it earns the read; everything after it proves it.
4. **Structure** — exactly 3 sub-points (never more), short phrases not paragraphs. This is what the paired visual carries — the caption should preview it, not duplicate it in full.
5. **Proof anchor** — a verified stat (Scout-sourced, softly attributed, no competitor name) or a genuine pattern claim grounded in real sector experience. Never both stacked back-to-back; pick one.
6. **So-what / CTA** — forward-looking implication, closed with a genuine engagement question. No outbound links in the body (LinkedIn suppresses reach on link-posts) — links go in the first comment if ever needed.
7. **Hashtags** — 3–5, at the very end, never inline. Mix: one branded (if applicable), one practice-area, one sector.

**Mechanical rules:** short paragraphs (1–2 sentences), line break between almost every thought, no walls of text.

**Coherence rule:** the hook's promise and the body's delivery must match exactly. If the hook names three things, the body must name those same three things, in the same order, and the close should echo them once more.

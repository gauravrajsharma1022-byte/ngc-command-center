## LinkedIn Caption

Seven signals are converging on telecom customer experience right now. They collapse into two structural gaps — and one operating model already demonstrated in a live deployment: 50-70% fewer billing calls, 15-25% less churn.

CX dashboards have never been fuller.

Billing-related call volumes haven't moved.

And the loyalty operators are investing to build isn't showing up in the numbers.

Telecom operators have the data, the AI, and the investment intent — but until the operating model closes the loop from measurement to action, none of it converts to loyalty.

Seven signals sit behind this pattern. They collapse into two structural gaps — and one operating model that closes them.

**1. Data without a closed loop**

A recent CX study of German companies found 96% of companies measure customer satisfaction — but only 20% convert that measurement into financial impact. The gap it illustrates — near-universal measurement, near-absent commercial translation — is the point, not the geography.

Asked why, 42% point to rigid internal process as the top barrier, ahead of insufficient investment willingness, cited by 37%.

The gap closes when measurement has somewhere to land, not just somewhere to report.

**2. Intent without ownership**

The same study found 83% of CX experts expect CX investment to grow. Only 54% have a dedicated team responsible for it.

Industry surveys separately show most telecom consumers report no binding loyalty to their primary provider.

The gap closes when that spend lands with someone accountable for what it produces.

**3. The operating model that actually closes the gap**

An eleven-organisation consortium — including Vodafone, TELUS and Verizon — ran one live deployment through TM Forum's Catalyst programme, the industry association behind telecom's shared process standards.

The care model shifted from reactive, CRM-driven queues to concierge, orchestration and domain-specific AI agents working billing and network events before a customer picks up the phone.

Network performance stopped sitting behind the CX function. It became the CX product itself.

In that same deployment: 50-70% fewer billing calls, 15-25% less churn, alongside a 15% drop in operating cost and a 5% lift in upsell and cross-sell revenue.

It's one consortium's proof of concept, not an industry average — but it's the clearest evidence yet of what an operating model produces once it's built to close the loop.

Seven signals. Two structural gaps: data without a closed loop, intent without ownership. One operating model that closes them.

Dashboards and a bold AI roadmap aren't what separate the operators closing these gaps from the ones still measuring them.

It's an operating model built to close the loop from measurement to action — and keep closing it long after the first results land.

Where's your gap — measurement or ownership? And is there an operating model in place to actually close it?
